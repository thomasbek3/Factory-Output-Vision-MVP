Exception Triage gives the clearest answer to the daily ops question: what needs attention, who owns it, and what happens next. Option 1 is the safest conventional alternative.

Current screen

Why it feels flat

The page has the right data, but nearly every element has the same visual weight. The result is difficult to scan and slow to operate.

  • Five equal KPI cards dominate the first viewport.
  • Support requests consume too much horizontal space.
  • The oldest queue item is visible but not actionable.
  • Reviewer actions are cryptic icon buttons.
  • There is no clear daily priority or next action.
Option 01

Operations Briefing

A balanced overview with queue health, worker support, and the reviewer roster visible together. Familiar, polished, and easy to implement incrementally.

Best for

General operations leads who need one stable home screen.

Tradeoff

Still dashboard-oriented rather than task-first.

Open full size
Option 02

Queue Command Center

Makes assignment throughput the primary job. The queue owns the canvas while support and reviewer capacity remain visible in a fixed operational sidebar.

Best for

High-volume review operations and SLA management.

Tradeoff

More complex and less useful before queue volume grows.

Open full size
Option 03

Daylight Operations

A calm light-theme workspace for long daytime shifts. It separates attention items from performance data and keeps the roster easy to scan.

Best for

Office teams working primarily in bright environments.

Tradeoff

Less visually connected to the current dark product.

Open full size
Option 04 Recommended

Exception Triage

Organizes the home screen around the issues that need action: overdue work, missing setup, support, and reviewer readiness. Every exception has an owner and next command.

Best for

Daily operating discipline and fast issue resolution.

Tradeoff

Requires reliable severity and ownership rules.

Open full size
Option 05

Workforce Board

Treats each reviewer as an operational lane, combining current work, shift activity, support, and readiness in one place.

Best for

Hands-on team leads managing a small reviewer workforce.

Tradeoff

Will need a different pattern once the team grows.
